Fort Hood is one of the biggest pieces of huntable ground in Central Texas, and a lot of soldiers stationed here hunt it. Here's how it works, and when private land is the better call.

## Hunting on post

Fort Hood runs hunting through **iSportsman** at fthood.isportsman.net. What its permit page says:

- You need a **Fort Hood hunting permit**, bought through your iSportsman account. For the current season it's $90, or $20 for a youth permit. Hunting permits expire Aug. 31 every year. All sales are final.
- Every adult and minor has to complete the **UXO safety briefing**, video and quiz, every year before getting a permit. It's a training area. There's unexploded ordnance out there.
- You're still responsible for holding a **valid Texas hunting license** and anything else state law requires.
- iSportsman also handles check-in and check-out.

Eligibility and area rules are set by the installation, and they change with training. Read the current Fort Hood hunting regulation and the iSportsman pages before you plan anything.

## Why people come to us instead

Post hunting is a good deal, and we're not going to talk anyone out of it. But it has limits, and most of them come down to the Army needing the ground:

- **Training comes first.** Area access is set by the installation, so the spot you scouted may not be open the weekend you're free.
- **You do the work.** Scouting, stands, calling, all of it, on ground you may not know.

A guided hunt on private land near Killeen gets you a fixed date, a guide who knows the ground, and guns and ammo if you need them. The price on the page is the price whether you bring your own gear or not.
